Tensor Derivation of the Geodesic from Los Angeles to Tokyo on a Spherical Earth

Purpose

This note derives the geodesic equations used by the accompanying SymPy and SciPy code. The derivation begins with the metric tensor of a sphere, computes the Christoffel symbols, forms the tensor geodesic equation, and then states the boundary-value problem for a path from Los Angeles to Tokyo.

Coordinate System

Use \(x^1=\phi,\qquad x^2=\lambda,\) where $\phi$ is latitude and $\lambda$ is longitude. A sphere of radius $R$ is parametrized by

\[\mathbf r(\phi,\lambda) = R\begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi\cos\lambda\\ \cos\phi\sin\lambda\\ \sin\phi \end{pmatrix}\]

The coordinate basis vectors are

\(\mathbf e\_\phi = \frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial\phi} = R\begin{pmatrix} -\sin\phi\cos\lambda\\ -\sin\phi\sin\lambda\\ \cos\phi \end{pmatrix}\) and

$$\mathbf e_\lambda

\frac{\partial\mathbf r}{\partial\lambda} = R\begin{pmatrix} -\cos\phi\sin\lambda
\cos\phi\cos\lambda
0 \end{pmatrix} $$

Metric Tensor

The metric components are

\[g_{ij}=\mathbf e_i\cdot\mathbf e_j\]

Therefore,

\[g_{\phi\phi}=R^2,\qquad g_{\phi\lambda}=0,\qquad g_{\lambda\lambda}=R^2\cos^2\phi\]

Thus, \(\boxed{ g_{ij} = \begin{pmatrix} R^2&0\\ 0&R^2\cos^2\phi \end{pmatrix} }\)

and

\[\boxed{ ds^2=R^2d\phi^2+R^2\cos^2\phi\,d\lambda^2 }\]

This corresponds to the SymPy construction

metric = (
    R_symbol**2 * TensorProduct(dphi, dphi)
    + R_symbol**2 * cos(phi)**2
      * TensorProduct(dlam, dlam)
)

The inverse metric is

\[\boxed{ g^{ij} = \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{1}{R^2}&0\\[6pt] 0&\dfrac{1}{R^2\cos^2\phi} \end{pmatrix} }\]

Christoffel Symbols

The Christoffel symbols of the second kind are

\[\boxed{ \Gamma^k_{ij} = \frac12g^{k\ell} \left( \partial_i g_{j\ell} +\partial_j g_{i\ell} -\partial_\ell g_{ij} \right) }\]

Only one metric component depends on the coordinates: \(g_{\lambda\lambda}=R^2\cos^2\phi\)

Therefore, \(\partial_\phi g_{\lambda\lambda} =-2R^2\sin\phi\cos\phi\)

and all other metric derivatives vanish.

Calculation of $\Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\lambda}$

Set $k=\phi$ and $i=j=\lambda$. Since the metric is diagonal, only $\ell=\phi$ contributes:

\[\Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\lambda} =-\frac12g^{\phi\phi}\partial_\phi g_{\lambda\lambda}\]

Substitution gives \(\Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\lambda} =-\frac12\frac1{R^2} \left(-2R^2\sin\phi\cos\phi\right)\)

so

\[\boxed{ \Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\lambda} =\sin\phi\cos\phi =\frac12\sin(2\phi). }\]

Calculation of $\Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda}$

Only $\ell=\lambda$ contributes: \(\Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda} =\frac12g^{\lambda\lambda}\partial_\phi g_{\lambda\lambda}\)

Therefore, \(\Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda} =\frac12\frac{1}{R^2\cos^2\phi} \left(-2R^2\sin\phi\cos\phi\right)\)

which gives \(\boxed{ \Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda}=-\tan\phi }\)

By symmetry of the Levi–Civita connection, \(\boxed{ \Gamma^\lambda_{\lambda\phi} =\Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda} =-\tan\phi }\)

Thus, the only nonzero Christoffel symbols are \(\boxed{ \Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\lambda}=\sin\phi\cos\phi, \qquad \Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda} =\Gamma^\lambda_{\lambda\phi} =-\tan\phi }\)

In the code, SymPy obtains them from

Gamma = metric_to_Christoffel_2nd(metric)

Tensor Geodesic Equation

A geodesic satisfies \(\boxed{ \frac{d^2x^k}{dt^2} +\Gamma^k_{ij} \frac{dx^i}{dt} \frac{dx^j}{dt}=0 }\)

Equivalently, \(\boxed{\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0}\)

Here, \(\gamma(t)= \begin{pmatrix} \phi(t)\\ \lambda(t) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \dot\gamma=\dot\phi\,\mathbf e_\phi+\dot\lambda\,\mathbf e_\lambda\)

Latitude Equation

For $k=\phi$, the fully expanded equation is \(\ddot\phi +\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\dot\phi^2 +\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\lambda}\dot\phi\dot\lambda +\Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\phi}\dot\lambda\dot\phi +\Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\lambda}\dot\lambda^2 =0\)

The first three Christoffel symbols vanish, so \(\ddot\phi +\Gamma^\phi_{\lambda\lambda}\dot\lambda^2=0\)

Hence,

\[\boxed{ \ddot\phi+\sin\phi\cos\phi\,\dot\lambda^2=0 }\]

or

\[\boxed{ \ddot\phi=-\sin\phi\cos\phi\,\dot\lambda^2 }\]

This corresponds to

dy[2] = -gamma_phi_ll * lam_dot**2

Longitude Equation

For $k=\lambda$,

\[\begin{aligned} \ddot\lambda &+\Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\phi}\dot\phi^2 +\Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda}\dot\phi\dot\lambda\\ &+\Gamma^\lambda_{\lambda\phi}\dot\lambda\dot\phi +\Gamma^\lambda_{\lambda\lambda}\dot\lambda^2=0 \end{aligned}\]

The only nonzero terms are the two mixed terms. Thus,

\[\ddot\lambda -\tan\phi\,\dot\phi\dot\lambda -\tan\phi\,\dot\lambda\dot\phi=0\]

so

\[\boxed{ \ddot\lambda-2\tan\phi\,\dot\phi\dot\lambda=0 }\]

or

\[\boxed{ \ddot\lambda=2\tan\phi\,\dot\phi\dot\lambda }\]

This corresponds to

dy[3] = -2.0 * gamma_lam_pl * phi_dot * lam_dot

because $\Gamma^\lambda_{\phi\lambda}=-\tan\phi$.

First-Order System Used by the Numerical Solver

Introduce \(y_1=\phi,\qquad y_2=\lambda,\qquad y_3=\dot\phi,\qquad y_4=\dot\lambda\)

Then the second-order tensor equations become

\[\boxed{ \begin{aligned} \dot y_1&=y_3\\ \dot y_2&=y_4\\ \dot y_3&=-\sin y_1\cos y_1\,y_4^2\\ \dot y_4&=2\tan y_1\,y_3y_4 \end{aligned} }\]

This is the system passed to scipy.integrate.solve_bvp.

Los Angeles-to-Tokyo Boundary-Value Problem

Using city-center coordinates, \(\begin{aligned} \phi_{\mathrm{LA}}&=34.0522^\circ, &\lambda_{\mathrm{LA}}&=-118.2437^\circ,\\ \phi_{\mathrm{Tokyo}}&=35.6895^\circ, &\lambda_{\mathrm{Tokyo}}&=139.6917^\circ \end{aligned}\)

For a continuous Pacific-going coordinate branch, Tokyo’s longitude is unwrapped (subtract $360^\circ$ from Tokyo’s longitude) as \(139.6917^\circ\equiv -220.3083^\circ\)

The boundary conditions are

\[\boxed{ \begin{aligned} \phi(0)&=\phi_{\mathrm{LA}}\\ \lambda(0)&=\lambda_{\mathrm{LA}}\\ \phi(1)&=\phi_{\mathrm{Tokyo}}\\ \lambda(1)&=\lambda_{\mathrm{Tokyo}} \end{aligned} }\]

The corresponding code is

return np.array([
    ya[0] - phi_A,
    ya[1] - lam_A,
    yb[0] - phi_B,
    yb[1] - lam_B,
])

The initial direction is not prescribed. Instead, the boundary-value solver finds $\dot\phi(0)$ and $\dot\lambda(0)$ such that the geodesic beginning in Los Angeles reaches Tokyo.

Distance Recovered from the Metric

Once $\phi(t)$ and $\lambda(t)$ are known, \(ds^2=R^2d\phi^2+R^2\cos^2\phi\,d\lambda^2\)

Dividing by $dt^2$ gives \(\left(\frac{ds}{dt}\right)^2 =R^2\left(\dot\phi^2+\cos^2\phi\,\dot\lambda^2\right)\)

Thus, \(\boxed{ \frac{ds}{dt} =R\sqrt{\dot\phi^2+\cos^2\phi\,\dot\lambda^2} }\) and the total length is \(\boxed{ L=R\int_0^1 \sqrt{\dot\phi^2+\cos^2\phi\,\dot\lambda^2}\,dt }\) The implementation is

metric_speed = R_KM * np.sqrt(
    phi_dot**2
    + np.cos(phi_path)**2 * lam_dot**2
)

distance_km = np.trapz(metric_speed, tau_plot)

For the numerical solution, \(\boxed{L\approx8815.473\ \mathrm{km}}\)

The path reaches a maximum latitude of approximately \(\boxed{47.952^\circ\ \mathrm{N}}\)

Calculation Chain

The complete tensor-based calculation is \(\mathbf r(\phi,\lambda) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_{ij} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Gamma^k_{ij} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0\)

\[\Longrightarrow\quad \begin{cases} \ddot\phi+\sin\phi\cos\phi\,\dot\lambda^2=0\\ \ddot\lambda-2\tan\phi\,\dot\phi\dot\lambda=0 \end{cases}\] \[\Longrightarrow\quad \text{solve the Los Angeles-to-Tokyo boundary conditions} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad L\approx8815.473\ \mathrm{km}\]

This demonstrates that the route is obtained from the metric tensor and its Levi–Civita connection rather than by directly inserting the known great-circle distance formula $L=R\theta$.
